How the AGL Platform Actually Works

A behind-the-scenes look at what happens when you log in, play a match, and log out — and why your AG Champion's code stays on your Hugging Face account the whole time.

Model Repo or Dataset Repo — What Goes Where?

Hugging Face accounts can hold several kinds of repos. Here's what each one is used for.

Model Repo (required)

Holds your trained weights, code, and config files. The platform downloads this into a private cache and runs it inside an isolated Docker sandbox to generate moves.

Dataset Repo

Optional. Used for any supporting data files your model needs at inference time (e.g. opening books, zone databases).

In short: your Model repo's files are cached locally and executed inside a locked-down Docker sandbox on the platform's own infrastructure — no network access, read-only filesystem, and strict CPU/memory/time limits. AGL never needs your code to be a live, always-on server.

How Your AI Actually Plays

A common question: "When I play, where does my model actually run?" It runs in a sandboxed Docker container on the platform's own servers — not on a Space you have to keep alive.

When you register your Model repo, the platform downloads a copy into a private, per-user cache directory. During a match, each move is generated by launching a fresh, hardened Docker container that mounts your model files read-only, has no network access, and is destroyed immediately after producing a move.

  • ✓Your repo is verified for ownership before it's cached — never used without your consent.
  • ✓Inference happens inside an isolated, resource-limited container on AGL's own servers — no external hosting required on your part.
  • ✓The sandbox has no network access and a read-only filesystem, so your code can't reach out or persist changes.

When You Log In

Logging in prepares a lightweight, private session workspace just for you — nothing about your AG Champions is downloaded or copied at this point either.

  • ▸A private workspace is set up in the background for your account, scoped to Chess and Breakthrough.
  • ▸The platform re-checks that your registered repos are still reachable and cached, so you're warned early if something's broken before a match starts.
  • ▸None of this touches your model weights or code — it's bookkeeping, not a download.
2

While You Play — How a Move Is Generated

Every single move, for both players, is generated live — nothing is precomputed or cached from a prior download.

Board position sent → Docker sandbox runs your model's get_move → Move validated → Game state updated
  • ▸Each request carries a strict time budget. If your model responds within it, the move is used as soon as it arrives (or after the configured "thinking time," whichever is longer).
  • ▸If your model is slow to respond, the platform waits up to the configured sandbox timeout — it doesn't forfeit immediately.
  • ▸Only if the sandbox times out or errors does the platform fall back to a random legal move, so the game keeps going instead of stalling.
3

When You Log Out

The private workspace created at login is torn down completely — it doesn't linger after your session ends.

  • ▸Your session workspace and any temporary bookkeeping files created for it are removed.
  • ▸Your actual AG Champion — its code, weights, and data — is completely unaffected, since none of it ever lived on the platform's servers in the first place. It's still exactly where you deployed it, on Hugging Face.
  • ▸Your Elo, match history, and registered repo links are saved permanently in your profile — only the ephemeral session data is cleared.

Why It's Built This Way

Your IP stays yours

Your model runs inside a locked-down, network-isolated container that's destroyed after every move — there's nothing for other players or the platform to leak or expose.

Fair, identical treatment

Every AG Champion — from a beginner's random-move bot to a Grandmaster-rated neural net — is called the same way, over the same protocol, with the same time budget.

Nothing persists between sessions

Each login starts from a clean slate. There's no stale cache from a previous match that could cause unexpected behavior in a new one.

You stay in control

Update your Model repo anytime (outside tournaments) and the next match re-caches and runs your latest code automatically.
